# Foundations of Positive Psychology and Social Influence: A Comprehensive Study Guide

This study guide provides a detailed synthesis of the work of three influential psychologists—Dacher Keltner, Martin Seligman, and Robert Cialdini—as well as the overarching framework of positive psychology. It explores the scientific study of well-being, the mechanics of human influence, and the evolutionary functions of prosocial emotions.

---

## I. Core Concepts and Theoretical Frameworks

### 1. Positive Psychology and Well-Being
Positive psychology is the scientific study of the conditions and processes that contribute to positive psychological states, such as contentment and joy, as well as the development of positive relationships and institutions.

#### The PERMA Model
Developed by Martin Seligman, the PERMA model outlines five independent elements that contribute to human flourishing:
*   **P – Positive Emotion:** Subjective assessments of feeling good.
*   **E – Engagement:** The presence of a "flow state," which can only be measured subjectively.
*   **R – Relationships:** The presence of friends, family, intimacy, and social connections.
*   **M – Meaning:** Belonging to and serving something larger than oneself.
*   **A – Achievement:** Accomplishment pursued for its own sake, even in the absence of positive emotion or meaning.

#### Character Strengths and Virtues
Seligman and Christopher Peterson developed a "positive" counterpart to the *DSM* called *Character Strengths and Virtues*. They distilled six core virtues valued across cultures and millennia:
1.  **Wisdom and Knowledge**
2.  **Courage**
3.  **Humanity**
4.  **Justice**
5.  **Temperance** (includes forgiveness, humility, prudence, and self-regulation)
6.  **Transcendence**

### 2. Theories of Power and Social Class
Dacher Keltner’s research investigates how social structures and internal states influence behavior.

*   **Approach/Inhibition Theory of Power:** Suggests that acquiring power has a disinhibiting effect. Power can lead individuals to disregard the social consequences of their actions.
*   **Social Class Influence:** Research by Keltner, Paul Piff, and Michael Kraus indicates that individuals from privileged backgrounds are more likely to exhibit unethical behavior (e.g., cheating, ignoring pedestrians), feel less compassion, and attribute success to their own superior traits rather than external factors.
*   **The Jen Ratio:** A concept explored in Keltner’s *Born to Be Good*, referring to the relationship between actions that bring out the good in others versus those that bring out the bad. A higher ratio is directly linked to greater happiness.

### 3. The Science of Prosocial Emotions
Keltner is a foundational figure in studying emotions that enable human survival through cooperation.

*   **Awe:** Defined as an emotion that influences moral, spiritual, and aesthetic lives. Keltner identifies the "eight wonders of life" where awe is found: moral beauty, nature, moving in unison, music, visual art, spirituality, big ideas, and life/death. Awe helps individuals integrate into communities by inspiring a collective self.
*   **Social Functions of Emotion:** Emotions like embarrassment, shame, love, and gratitude enable individuals to form the relationships vital for survival, including parent-child attachments and romantic partnerships.

### 4. Learned Helplessness and Optimism
Martin Seligman’s early work focused on the perceived absence of control.

*   **Learned Helplessness:** A psychological condition where humans or animals learn to act helplessly after experiencing an inability to avoid adverse situations, even when they later gain the power to change their circumstances. Seligman linked this to clinical depression.
*   **Learned Optimism:** The counterpart to helplessness, focusing on how changing one's attributional style can lead to better mental health outcomes.

### 5. The Principles of Persuasion
Robert Cialdini identifies specific psychological "short-cuts" used in marketing and social influence.

| Principle | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Reciprocity** | The internal pull to repay what another person has provided us. |
| **Commitment and Consistency** | The desire to appear consistent with what we have already done or said. |
| **Social Proof** | Looking to others to determine correct behavior, especially in uncertain situations. |
| **Authority** | The tendency to obey or be influenced by those perceived as experts or leaders. |
| **Liking** | Being more easily persuaded by people we know, like, or find similar to ourselves. |
| **Scarcity** | Assigning more value to opportunities that are less available. |
| **Unity** | The principle that the more we identify ourselves with others, the more they influence us. |

## III. Essay Prompts for Deeper Exploration

1.  **The Evolution of Seligman’s Career:** Trace the transition of Martin Seligman’s research from "Learned Helplessness" and depression to "Positive Psychology" and "Well-Being Theory." How did his early experiments with dogs inform his later theories on human flourishing?
2.  **The Paradox of Power:** Analyze Keltner’s "Power Paradox." How can the same traits that help a person gain influence eventually lead to their social and ethical "atrophy" once power is attained? Use the Approach/Inhibition Theory in your response.
3.  **Social Functions of "Negative" Emotions:** While positive psychology focuses on well-being, Keltner argues that emotions like embarrassment and shame serve vital social functions. Discuss how these and other "prosocial" emotions facilitate human survival and relationship building.
4.  **Ethics in Psychological Research:** Discuss the ethical critiques raised regarding the foundational experiments of both Seligman (dog shocks) and Keltner (solitary confinement research). How have these researchers addressed societal relevance and ethics in their later work?
5.  **The Mechanics of Persuasion:** Using Robert Cialdini’s seven principles, analyze a real-world scenario (such as a political campaign or a marketing strategy). How do these principles exploit human psychological tendencies to achieve specific outcomes?

## IV. Glossary of Important Terms

*   **Awe:** A distinct emotional state triggered by vastness and "wonders," such as moral beauty or nature, that inspires cooperative tendencies and a collective self-representation.
*   **Compassion:** A prosocial emotion rooted in evolutionary survival that facilitates parent-child attachments and group cohesion.
*   **Flow State:** A state of total engagement in an activity; a key component of the "Engagement" element in the PERMA model.
*   **Learned Helplessness:** A condition where an individual perceives a total absence of control over outcomes, leading to a refusal to attempt escape from harmful circumstances.
*   **Positive Organizational Scholarship:** The application of positive psychology principles within workplace and institutional settings.
*   **Prosocial Emotions:** Emotions such as love, gratitude, and compassion that enable humans to form survival-essential relationships.
*   **Reciprocity:** A principle of influence based on the social obligation to return favors or gifts.
*   **Social Proof:** A psychological phenomenon where people mirror the actions of others in an attempt to reflect correct behavior in a given situation.
*   **Unity Principle:** Cialdini's seventh principle of persuasion, stating that influence is maximized when the persuader and the target share a common identity.
*   **Valence and Arousal:** Traditional constructs used to represent emotion, which Keltner’s "data-driven" approach challenges in favor of distinct emotion concepts like "awe" or "sympathy."
